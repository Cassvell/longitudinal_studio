	\begin{abstract}
		
		The present study seeks to analyze longitudinally, the magnetic fluctuations associated with the disturbed dynamo electric field (DDEF) using 11 observatories encircling the globe at mid-low latitudes. The event to be studied is the geomagnetic storm known as the Saint Patrick event due to his main phase was developing during that celebration (march 17th, 2015).In the present studio, we perform a wavelet spectrum in combination with cross-wavelet technique and a semblance analysis studio in order to isolate the DDEF magnetic signatures.  
		\\
		
		\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Disturbed dynamo, wavelet
	\end{abstract}

	\section{Introduction}
	\label{sec:intro}
		
	During geomagnetic storms (GS), one of the responses which arise in the ionosphere is the driving of electric currents. In the case of mid-low geomagnetic latitude ranges, we highlight the presence of the Disturbed dynamo (Ddyn) and the disturbed polar current number 2 (DP2). \\
	
	The selected GS, known as St. Patrick's event is known as the strongest GS event during $24^{th}$ solar cycle. Interplanetary data was obtained from the OMNIweb database (\url{https://omniweb.gsfc.nasa.gov/form/omni_min.html}) meanwhile the geomagnetic indices were downloaded from ISGI database (\url{https://isgi.unistra.fr/data_download.php}).\\
		\subsection{Interplanetary Observations}
	
 	From Figure \ref{fig:ip} we present a briefly description of the trigger event which lead to the St. Patrick's GS. A more detailed description of the trigger event is reviewed by \cite{stpatrick_ref}. \\
 	
 	The panel (a) show the Interplanetary magnetic field $B_T$ (black line) and its component  $B_Z$ (red line). Panel (b) and (c) on the other hand, present some properties of the Solar Wind, being the speed $V_X$ (considered positive in the anti-sunward direction, black line), the Proton Temperature (blue line) and Proton Density (orange line) so as the Interplanetary Electric Field which results from $V_X \times B_Z$. Finally, in the panel (e) we present the planetary geomagnetic response represented by the geomagnetic index SYM-H (panel e, green line). \\
	
	The trigger event was highlighted using three vertical dashed lines within the panels (a)-(d) as it is shown in Figure \ref{fig:ip}. The first dashed lines (left to right) indicates the arriving of the Interplanetary shock (IPS) at 4:30 UT detected at L1. The IPS signature is clearly seen as a step effect seen in $B_T$ and $B_Z$ so as in Solar Wind Speed $V_X$ and $n_P$ meanwhile is less notorious in $T_P$. The arriving of the IPS at earths near space leaded to a compression effect on the day side's magnetosphere resulting in a temporal enhancement of Earth's  H component magnetic field. The enhancement is seeing in SYM-H index, between 04:30 and 07:00 UT in panel (e), highlighted by a light gray shading and is known as Sudden Storm Commencement (SSC).\\
	
	Later, it came the arrival of a CME's sheath, identified by the highly fluctuating behavior on $B_T$ and $B_Z$, so as an enhancement in $n_P$ and $T$, characteristics of a Sheath. It is important to note that $B_Z$ component shows change of direction southward between 6:00 and 9:00 UT with minimum values around -18 nT. Consequently, there is a descending tendency in SYM-H index (panel e) which marks the beginning of the main phase (MP) until 9:00 UT when the $B_Z$ component changed its direction northward. The minimum value reached during this period is ~-80 nT. On Panel (f), we observe the beginning activity on the Injection function Q(t). This parameter can be used as an indicator of the energy input on ring current during reconnexion mechanism.\\
	
	Finally, we indicate the crossing of the CME near 14:00 UT (marked by the second dashed line). As indicators we highlight a less fluctuating behavior on $B_T$ and $B_Z$ so as a decreasing Temperature in panel (d). With the arriving of the CME, a new period of southward $B_Z$  is present, leading to a second period of magnetic reconnection. Hence we observe a second decrease in SYM-H index from 12 UT until 23:00 UT reaching a minimum value of -228 nT. The whole MP period is highlighted by a gray shading at panel (e). In panel (f), we highlight with light gray shading the period of interest where we observe higher Injection due to the MP meanwhile. On the other hand, we show remarkable periods of Injection with a darker gray shading. Observe that the Injection is at its highest at the beginning of the second period of MP which occurs between 13:00 and 16:30. We expect higher amplitude fluctuations related to $H_PPEF$ during those periods highlighted with dark gray shading. According to \cite{klimenko_ddyn}, $H_{PPEF}$ fluctuations can be also induced during sudden and sharp changing northward $B_Z$, hence we highlighted also periods of sharp decay on Q(t). When $B_Z$ turns northward again which represent the beginning of recovery phase and is highlighted by the third dashed vertical line.\\

	\section{Methodology}
	\label{sec:method}
	
	
	\subsection{Magnetic fluctuations of ionospheric origin}
	According to \cite{1969intro_to_iono_p, l_handbook_geof_sw_Geom_field, baseline_Gjerloev, vanKampt}, the recordings of a local magnetometer can be considered as a linear combination of different geomagnetic sources due to the superposition law:
	\begin{equation}
		H_{loc} = H_{SQ} + H_0 + H_{MT} + H_{I},
	\end{equation}
	
	\noindent where $H_{SQ}$ is the diurnal variation and $H_0$ is the monthly baseline. Thus, $\Delta H = H_{loc} - (H_{SQ} + H_0)$ is the local geomagnetic response during a GS. We deepen about how we model the regular variation in the Appendix \ref{a1}. $H_{MT}$ on the other hand are the variations induced due to the magnetospheric current intensification during the GS. Finally, $H_I$ is the magnetic variations driven by ionospheric currents activity during a GS.\\
	
	According to \cite{ddyn2005, amorymazaudier_2017, amory2020_filtros}, for mid-low magnetic latitudes we can approach roughly enough $H_{MT}$ as $H_{MT} \approx SYM - H \cdot cos(\lambda)$, being $\lambda$ the geomagnetic latitude of each Obs, shown in column 6 of Table \ref{tab:obs}. Thus $H_I$ can be approached by:
	
	\begin{equation}
		\label{eq:H_I}
		H_I = H_{DDEF} + H_{PPEF} \approx H - (H_{SQ} + H_0 + SYM-H \cdot cos(\lambda)),
	\end{equation}
	
	\noindent being $H_{DDEF}$ and $H_{PPEF}$ the magnetic field associated with the disturbed dynamo electric fields and the prompt penetration electric fields respectively.\\
	
	
	\subsection{Time-Frequency Analysis}
	In order to isolate $H_{DDEF}$ and $H_{PPEF}$ from each other, we can proceed from the consideration that both ionospheric currents drive quasi periodic fluctuations \cite{nishida_68_fluctuations, blanc_ddyn, amory2020_filtros}. Hence we begin with a Fourier Analysis, setting passband filters for fluctuations of frequencies about $11.5 \mu Hz$ (periods ~24 h) to reconstruct $H_{DDEF}$. In case of $H_{PPEF}$ we set highpass filters for frequencies higher than $69.5 \mu Hz$ (periods ~4h).\\
	
	However, Fourier analysis only gives a frequency domain perspective. In order to analyze also the time domain, we perform wavelet techniques \citep{APracticalGuidetoWaveletAnalysis}, as done in \cite{amory_younas2021}, where they to compare two time series ($H_{SQ}$ and $H_I$) with similar band frequency fluctuations ($f \approx 1.15e-5 Hz$ or $T \approx 24 h$). Then, identifying which signal is more anti-correlated to $H_{SQ}$ and so, more correlated to $H_{DDEF}$ activity. More specifically, we performed a wavelet analysis ($W$), using the Morlet function as the mother wavelet. Hence we computed the wavelet spectrum ($|\psi_0|^2$) in order to observe the power of the signal related to $H_I$. As second stage, we computed the XWT as described in \cite{APracticalGuidetoWaveletAnalysis}:
	
	\begin{equation}
		\label{eq:xwt}
		W_n^{SQ,H_I}(s) = W_n^{H_{SQ}}(s) W_n^{H_I *}(s),
	\end{equation}
	
	\noindent being $s$ the scale of the wavelet transform and $n$ the index number of each time series. Within the right side of equation \ref{eq:xwt} $W_n^{H_{SQ}}(s)$ is the wavelet transform applied to the diurnal variation meanwhile $ W_n^{H_I *}(s)$ is the conjugate of the wavelet transform applied to $H_I$. According to \cite{cooper_semblance} the output of XWT, local phase $\theta$ can be used to compare two datasets based on correlations between their phase angles ($\theta$), as function of frequency. Then, we can define Semblance as $S = cos^n(\theta)$ where  $n$ is an odd integer number greater than 1. We set $n=9$ as tested in \cite{cooper_semblance}.\\ 
	
	
	$S$ alone however, does not provide the intensity of neither of the two signals but a correlation from -1 to 1. Values closer to 1 (-1) mean more correlation to $H_{SQ}$ ($H_{DDEF}$). The lack of amplitude also means that semblance is sensitive to noise. To overcome this issue, we use the amplitude $\alpha$, another XWT output, as scaling factor in order to enhance the signals related to $H_{SQ}$ and $H_{DDEF}$. Then Equation which describes the complete semblance analysis is:
	
	\begin{equation}
		\label{eq:semblance}
		S = \alpha \cdot cos ^n(\theta).
	\end{equation}
	
	 It is necessary to consider that the result of Equation \ref{eq:semblance} includes not only $H_{DDEF}$ signal, but also the signal related to $SQ$ and PRC \citep{amory2020_filtros}. In order to isolate completely $H_{DDEF}$ in the spectrogram, we normalize the result of Equation \ref{eq:semblance} for a range [-1 ,1] since it is easier to separate normalize signals. However, in this case we reverse the scale so values closer to 1 can be interpreted as $H_{DDEF}$. Considering that values closer to 0 are not related to $H_{SQ}$ nor $H_{DDEF}$, we take correlations higher than 0.5 as $H_{DDEF}$ so:\\
	
	\begin{equation}
		\label{eq:ddyn}
		\alpha \cdot S = \begin{cases}
			 0, \qquad \qquad	\text{ $S_i < 0.6$}\\
			 H_{DDEF}, \quad \text{ $S_i \geq 0.6 $}
		\end{cases}
	\end{equation}
	
	Having the wavelet spectrum $|\psi_0(n)|^2$ which give us the power of the signals and $\alpha \cdot anticorr(semblance)$ which highlights the signal related to $H_{DDEF}$, we can observe not only weather $H_{DDEF}$ variations are present during the event but also, their intensity. 
	
	\section{Results}

	In the following section we present the St. Patrick's GS geomagnetic response for each Obs of Tabla \ref{tab:obs}. 	\subsection{Geomanetic and Ionospheric response}

	In top panels within Figure \ref{fig:magdata} we show $\Delta H$ for each obs and compare them with the global indices SYM-H and ASYM-H (which represent a quantitative measure of ring current and partial ring current respectively). at bottom panels, we show the results of Equation \ref{eq:H_I} (red line) in comparison to $H_{SQ}$ modeled from each Obs (see Appendix \ref{a1} for more details). For each panel we decided to highlight local night time periods with a light gray shading set from 18:00 to 06:00 LT each day. That way is easier to indicate differences in local time for each Obs during and after the MP.\\
	
	We begin with the Obs from \textbf{Zone 1} (GUI and TAM plots) were, the first stage of the MP (triggered by the CME's sheath) happened while GUI and TAM were on day side. Second stage of MP (triggered by the CME) occurred partially on day side and night side, almost centered on dusk sector (18:00 LT). Both cases show an enhancement of $\Delta H$ near local dusk which is also consistent with an enhancement on ASYM-H activity, specially in TAM's local response. In GUI, $\Delta H$ reaches its minimum near 19:00 LT meanwhile TAM's minimum occurs at 18:00 LT. After their minimums, we observe that local magnetic response keeps enhanced until we reach the minimum at SYM-H which coincides with a second period of ASYM-H activity near 23:00 LT.\\ 
	
	In bottom panels we observe a sharp crest related to $H_{SQ}$ activity near noon meanwhile $H_{I (GUI, TAM)}$ present two responses. First, the most intense response which occurs during MP, can be related to both, PRC activity and $H_{PPEF}$ fluctuations with peak amplitudes of ~ -110 nT (GUI) and ~ -150 nT (TAM). These responses are present mostly around dusk sector, quite similar to ASYM-H activity, with TAM having a more intense activity. The other effect in which we are interested in, are fluctuations associated to $H_{DDEF}$. These  fluctuations are present after several hours to a day after the MP's ending according to \cite{ddyn2005}. Then could only consider probable $H_{DDEF}$ fluctuations when $H_I$ is at least one day after $\Delta H$ minimum. Another condition is that  $H_I$ fluctuations are approximately opposed to $H_{SQ}$. In other words, any time there is a maximum (minimum) on $H_{SQ}$, there is a minimum (maximum) in $H_I$. Hence we could consider as $H_I = H_{DDEF}$ between March $18^{th}$ and $21^{th}$.\\
	
	For JAI in \textbf{Zone 2}, it is noticeable that MP at day side both responses, global and local were pretty similar. After reaching dusk sector, local response gets a sharp enhancement compared with SYM-H with $\Delta H$ reaching its minimum at 21:00 LT. Similar to GUI and TAM, this enhancement is consistent with the sharp enhancement of ASYM-H.\\
	
	At bottom panel, between 18 and 22:30 there is the most intense activity from $H_I$ from which we expect higher fluctuations related to $H_{PPEF}$. During this period, there seems to be an almost inverse proportional behavior between ASYM-H in top panel and $H_I$, reaching a minimum value of ~-150 nT. From GUI and TAM toward JAI we observe a sharp positive enhancement of $H_I$ being more pronounced going eastward (near noon at TAM and near dawn at JAI). The case where $H_I \approx H_{DDEF}$ on the other hand, is probable to happen during March $19^{th}$ and March $26-28^{th}$. However, the last one mentioned would not be necessarily related to St. Patrick's GS.\\

	\subsection{Reconstruction of $\mathbf{H_{PPEF}}$ and $\mathbf{H_{DDEF}}$}

	Results of applying frequency filters to FFT($H_I$) are shown in Figure \ref{fig:recons}. Fluctuations $H_{PPEF}$ are represented with red lines meanwhile $H_{DDEF}$ are represented with black lines for each observatory. Similar to Figure \ref{fig:magdata}, we represent each local night time with gray shades.\\
	
	In \textbf{Zone 1} $H_{PPEF}$ present their fluctuations since the beginning of MP. However, the Sheath did not drive significant $H_{PPEF}$ fluctuations, compared to the CME. From the arrival of the CME, there is a tendency of enhancement from noon sector toward dusk sector. In both cases GUI and TAM there is a sharp fluctuation of -30 and -45 nT respectively. Then, a decrease tendency in amplitudes begin until midnight when its presented a new sharp pick in $H_{PPEF}$ of 34 and 60 nT for GUI and TAM respectively. This enhancement is consistent with the minimum of SYM-H and the end of reconnexion mechanism as seen in Figure \ref{fig:ip}. In the case of $H_{DDEF}$ fluctuations, from the previous Figure \ref{fig:magdata}, we concluded that they can be considered valid from march $18^{th}$. At the beginning, in both GUI and TAM, the maximum amplitude is -20 nT but these amplitudes decrease rapidly until march $21^{th}$. In the case of GUI however, there is a small enhancement between march $22^{th}$ and $23^{th}$.\\
	
	JAI in \textbf{Zone 2} presents a fluctuation tendency in $H_{PPEF}$ related to the CME during night hours being centered around midnight with an amplitude of ~40 nT meanwhile the maximum amplitude is 52 nT, near dawn (6:00 LT).  An enhancement of $H_{PPEF}$ is present close to local dusk with -41 nT of magnitude. $H_{DDEF}$ on the other hand presents a similar behavior as in Zone 1 with an initial amplitude about 20 nT during at march $18^th$, decreasing until march $21^{th}$.\\
	
	
	The \textbf{Zone 3} as the previous case, presents most of the $H_{PPEF}$ relevant fluctuations during night hours, including the fist two hours after dawn. $H_{PPEF}$ Fluctuations driven by the CME presents a sharp enhancement close to each local midnight within this zone. The Maximum fluctuation is around each local midnight, going from 50, 55, 47 and 30 nT for LZH, BMT, CYG and KAK respectively. After each local dawn, there is another enhancement of $H_{PPEF}$, then a sharp decrease tendency of amplitudes. \section{Discussion}

From Figures \ref{fig:magdata}, \ref{fig:recons} and \ref{fig:wavelets}, we observe consistent and similar results for the observatories of each Zone. In the case of \textbf{Zone 1} during which the MP phase happens mostly during day side hours except for the last 6 hours. We observe in Figure \ref{fig:magdata} that about 14:00 UT (15:00 LT in case of TAM) and enhancement of Local magnetic response which at the same time is consistent with ASYM-H enhancement. Considering that ASYM-H describes the asymmetric response of the ring current system, it takes into account the PCR contribution. top panels of Figure \ref{fig:magdata} shows that PRC influence local geomagnetic activity between 14:00 UT and 23:00 UT, specially at the 2 extreme regions which corresponds with the PCR terminals connected to the Birkeland current \cite{l_russell}.\\


Considering the shape of PRC, the contribution to $\Delta H_{loc}$ is not equal for each observatory as it can be observed in Figure \ref{fig:magdata}. As we go eastward through \textbf{Zone 2}, \textbf{Zone 3} and \textbf{Zone 4} (the last two having their MP during night hours), the contribution of PRC becomes less and less important for each local response as the observatory is farther away from the region of PRC activity during each local time. In \textbf{Zone 5} on the other hand, the observatories got some PRC enhancement but only during their last MP hours which coincided with their local dusk.\\ 

The way in which each local response got their respective enhancement (or lack of it) from PRC activity affected the result of Equation \ref{eq:H_I} for each observatory. Then, $H_I$ gets different amplitudes and behaviors for each Zone. In case of \textbf{Zone 1}, $H_I$ has high negative values, as local response is higher (more negative values) than the global response. On the other hand, from \textbf{Zone 2} to \textbf{Zone 4}, we observe an attenuation of $H_I$ within the period 14:00 - 19:00 UT which corresponds to the first and most enhanced activity of PRC. The reason is that the more eastern the observatory is placed, it will be farther from the PCR extreme terminal thus, getting less contribution from it. We observe also an enhancement of $H_I$ toward more positive values within the period of 21 - 03 LT, which corresponds with a more attenuated local response in comparison to SYM-H. On the other hand, observatories in \textbf{Zone 5}, while there is again an enhancement of more negative values near local dusk, when SYM-H has its minimum. This means that observatories within \textbf{Zone 5} received a sharp enhancement of PRC at the end of the MP.\\

Although is quite interesting the difference in $H_{DDEF}$ response between TEO and SJG, there is not an easy answer to explain this due to the lack of observatories between HON-TEO, and TEO-SJG to observe the longitudinal development within those sectors.\\

	\subsection{Partial Ring Current contribution}

From \cite{amory2020_filtros}, it is clear that the PRC presents an important contribution to local response so it may affect the result of Equation \ref{eq:H_I} and its resultant filtered signals. However, considering PRC limited longitudinal region of influence, we took the opportunity of analyzing its contribution to each observatory. From Figure \ref{fig:magdata}, it is shown a similar behavior between ASYMH index and $H_I$ in observatories like JAI, LZH and BMT from 12 UT to 18 UT during march $^{th}$. Thus we decided to carryout a correlation analysis for every observatory.\\ 

Taking the $H_I$ and ASYM-H from 12 to 18 UT of march $17^{th}$, we computed a Pearson correlation in two stages: first, correlation ASYM-H vs $H_I$. Second, ASYM-H vs $H_I$ while removing $H_{PPEF}$ fluctuations from $H_I$ through a low pass filter. The results are shown within the Table \ref{tab:corr}. Each row represent each of the two correlation stages.From this Table we find that only half of the observatories present a good correlation, being JAI, LZH and TEO the those ones with the highest correlation. Interestingly enough, at the beginning of this period chosen, JAI and TEO were also the closest observatories to the dusk and dawn sector respectively. On the other hand, after removing $H_{PPEF}$ fluctuations from $H_I$ through a low pass filter we observe that the correlation is lost at these observatories, meanwhile in the others, the dispersion is enhanced. \\


From the correlation result, we plot the dispersion of ASYMH vs $H_I$ as seen in Figure \ref{fig:corr}. Gray diamonds represent the first correlation meanwhile the black solid line is a linear fit using least squares technique and the 2 dashed lines represent a 95$\%$ confidence interval. These fit models were computed for JAI, LZH, BMT, CYG and TEO; each one having a $R^2 > 0.5$. \\

The implications of this analysis are: 

\begin{enumerate}
	\item PRC contribution might be significant weather the Observatory is locally placed near the terminals of PRC during MP.
	\item $H_{PPEF}$ fluctuations are highly related to PRC activity.
	\item From Figure \ref{fig:recons}, we observe that $H_{PPEF}$ fluctuation amplitudes are enhanced by PRC activity depending on the Observatory distance to any of dawn / dusk sectors.
\end{enumerate}

	\section{conclusions}
	
	The aim of this project was analyzing longitudinally the magnetic fluctuations driven by ionospheric currents during an intense geomagnetic storm, such as the St. Patrick's event on march the $17^{th}$, in 2015. In order to achieve this, we used geomagnetic data from ground magnetometer observatories located at different time sectors.\\ 
	
	During this study we analyzed the magnetic signatures associated to Prompt Penetration Electric Fields (PPEF) and Disturbed Dynamo Electric Fields. Through a Fourier Analysis we carried out the reconstruction of $H_{PPEF}$ and $H_{DDEF}$ using high pass and pass band filters respectively. We Also performed Wavelet technique in order to highlight the $H_{DDEF}$ signal from any other source within the frequency band of 11.5 $\mu Hz$ ($Period \approx 24 h$).\\
	
	After reconstructing $H_{PPEF}$ for each observatory we came up with the following conclusions:\\
	
	\begin{itemize}
		\item 	For this study case, $H_{PPEF}$ present higher fluctuations are at night side rather than day side, specifically when a obs was crossing the dusk - midnight sector during the highest PRC activity according to ASYMH index. This was the case of TAM, JAI, LZH and BMT, which got higher thresholds of $H_{PPEF}$ amplitudes.
		
		\item The previous point is consistent with the correlation analysis, where the highest correlation between ASYMH and $H_I$ was reached for the same observatories of higher $H_{PPEF}$ thresholds (except for TAM).
		
		\item At the same time, removing $H_{PPEF}$ contribution from $H_I$ for the correlation, resulted in a loss of correlation. Altogether could mean that $H_{PPEF}$ amplitudes are highly enhanced due to PRC activity. 
		
		\item We also observe that some of the highest $H_{PPEF}$ amplitudes occur less than an hour the time window during which two reported substorms (onsets at 17:29 and 22:50 UT) by \cite{substorm_stpatrick, substorm_stpatrick2}.
		
		\item The previous point would imply that a substorm might also affect $H_{PPEF}$ amplitudes for observatories near dusk, midnight or dawn sector.
		
	\end{itemize}
	
	About $H_{DDEF}$, from both, Fourier and Semblance analysis, we conclude the following:
	
	\begin{itemize}
		\item TEO is the observatory where $H_{DDEF}$ presents a longer period of activity. TEO and GUI (which presents a briefly re intensification) are the obs present mostly present at day side during MP.\\
		
		\item On the other hand, \textbf{Zone 3 and 4} observatories show practically null $H_{DDEF}$ activity. This is consistent with the disturbed dynamo model \cite{blanc_ddyn}, where the drop of conductivity during night side so as the vanishing of Ionospheric E region means that DDEF are way less intense, so as $H_{DDEF}$.
		
		\item 
	\end{itemize}
	
	 
	The PRC contribution to each observatory depends on their longitudinal position during the highest PRC activity. Considering this, its contribution is higher at Observatories of Zones 1 and 2 meanwhile it has presence on Zone 5. On the contrary, Observatories from Zone 3 and 4 the ones with a minor contribution to their local response in terms of magnitude.\\

	\section{Processing magnetic data}
	\label{a1}
	%	\subsection{Processing magnetic data}
	It is important to remove the regular variations present within each magnetic register in order to preserve only the magnetic contributions in which we are interested. The main field $H_0$ which can be removed through modeling a daily monthly baseline, was approximated by computing either the daily mode or a center of a gaussian fit \citep{baseline_Gjerloev} for the local-night magnetic data $H[0:3](t)$. Then, we compute $H_0$ from the median of each daily value in march. To not consider daily values during disturbed days, we filtered them by adapting the procedure in \cite{vanKampt}. Hence, we swift a moving window, computing an hourly the Inter Quantil Range (IQR). To filter the disturbed days, we have to set a threshold of variation, where:
	\begin{equation}
		IQR_{med}^i > threshold = disturbed
	\end{equation}
	
	This threshold was set considering the distribution of the IQR picks, from selecting maximum IQR ($IQR_{max}$) by resampling data again using tri-hourly bins.\\
	
	To compute the diurnal variation, we looked for what we refer as local quiet days (LQD) for each station. Basically, as done in \cite{vanKampt}, we consider as LQD, the five days with minor daily IQR picks. Then, we compute the average of the H component data during those five quietest days:
	
	\begin{equation}
		\label{eq:lqd}
		<LDQ_H(t)> = \frac{1}{5} \sum_{i=1}^5 H_i(t) - <H_i[0:3](LT)>
	\end{equation}
	\noindent where we normalize the offset of each daily data by sustracting the average of the first 4 hours magnetic data in local time, as explained by the second term in the Equation \ref{eq:lqd}. Hence we model the diurnal variation $H_{SQ}$ from applying a low pass filter following the Equation (1) in \cite{vanKampt} to the Fourier Transform of the result in Equation \ref{eq:lqd}.
